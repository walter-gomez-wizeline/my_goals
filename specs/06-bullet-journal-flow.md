# Bullet Journal walkthrough — v0.6

## Foundation and adaptation

Method references: [official introduction](https://bulletjournal.com/blogs/faq) and [migration](https://bulletjournal.com/pages/migration), consulted on 2026-10-26. Index, future/monthly/daily logs, collections, and task/event/note capture structure the notebook. Review and migration support reconsidering commitments.

The following is the proposed application journey. Links replace physical page references. Shared accounts, search, in-progress state, subtasks, tables, and calculations are digital adaptations/extensions.

## 1. Open the journal

After authentication, the current day appears with access to index, month, future, and review. No goal creation or field configuration is requested. An empty journal is valid.

Verification: AC-05, AC-51, AC-61.

## 2. Capture something

Write text and choose task, event, or note; note is the proposed default for free capture. The date matches the open day. Write briefly or expand a note.

Illustrative example:

```text
October 26
• Review courses
○ Study group meeting
– I could concentrate better today
– Lunch: 650 kcal
```

The last note does not automatically generate a numeric record. Converting it into a form is optional and requires confirming values. AI text interpretation is outside the initial scope.

Verification: RF-02, AC-61, AC-63.

## 3. Open the index and create a collection

Create “Study,” “Food,” or any topic. Add new entries or link existing ones. Entries retain identity and remain accessible from their day and collections. The index finds topics without scanning every day.

Verification: AC-55 through AC-58.

## 4. Plan in the future log

Save a commitment for a later month, optionally with a day. It remains a task/event; planning is not completion. When the month arrives, it appears for review and explicit transfer to the monthly log.

Verification: AC-59, AC-62.

## 5. Consult month and day

The monthly log combines calendar and task list. References open original entries. The daily log grows with capture; no advance planning or filling empty days is required.

Verification: AC-13, AC-14, AC-60, AC-61.

## 6. Mark and organize

Completion shows X; discard shows strikethrough. Priority/inspiration are optional signifiers. Scheduling/migration shows a historical mark linked to the destination. Priority does not turn a note into a task.

In-progress state and subtasks become available with extensions without changing the basic marks' meaning.

Verification: RF-15, RF-08.

## 7. Review and reflect

Open a period to read tasks, notes, events, and records. Write what happened, worked, and needs adjustment. No goal or percentage is required. Monthly review also surfaces pending tasks and future commitments becoming current.

Verification: AC-66 through AC-68, RF-06.

## 8. Decide and migrate

For each pending item, keep, complete, discard, migrate to a month/collection, or schedule in the future log. The application shows origin/destination and saves history. It does not automatically move everything.

Saving free reflection and closing a pending-item review are separate actions. Only the latter requires decisions for the pending set explicitly selected for review.

Verification: RF-05, AC-65, AC-68.

## 9. Add tracking when needed

A collection can add a meal table, weight series, or course list and may link to a goal. Previous information remains accessible. Indicators are optional and their interpretation depends on their source.

Verification: RF-01, RF-03, RF-09, RF-12; examples E-01 through E-04.
